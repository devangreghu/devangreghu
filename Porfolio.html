Optimal Route Finder

Enter your hotel, get the smartest way to explore — real-time transit options, ticket prices, and optimal routes, calculated instantly from wherever you're staying.

Platform
Google AI Studio
Domain
Travel / Transit
Status
Live Prototype

The Problem

Travelers arriving at a new hotel often lose the first hour juggling maps, transit apps, and ticketing sites just to figure out where to go next and how to get there affordably.

The Approach

A single-input experience: enter your hotel or accommodation, and the app surfaces real-time transport options — schedules, ticket prices, and optimal routes — so the decision of "where next" arrives with the logistics already solved.

The Build

Built and shipped using Google AI Studio, prioritizing fast validation of the idea over writing infrastructure from scratch — a deliberate tool choice, not a shortcut.

What's Next

User testing on how people actually define "optimal" — fastest, cheapest, or fewest transfers — and whether the app should default to one or let users set their own priority.

The Problem

Nutrition labels are dense and hard to interpret quickly — most people can't tell whether a packaged product is actually healthy just by glancing at the ingredients or the numbers on the back.

The Approach

A single scan — camera, photo, or barcode — gives users a transparent 0–100 health score and a breakdown of ingredient-level risk, benchmarked against public guidelines from WHO, FSSAI, and ICMR/NIN, so the judgment isn't left to guesswork.

The Build

Built and shipped using Lovable, again prioritizing a fast, testable prototype over building label-scanning infrastructure from scratch.

What's Next

Validating scoring accuracy against a wider set of packaged foods, and testing whether users trust and act on the health score in real shopping situations.

Product Case Study · Subscription & Billing UX
Netflix: Reducing Accidental Plan Upgrades

A proposal to stop customers from unintentionally upgrading their plan just to keep watching on shared, concurrent-stream accounts.

Company
Netflix (Independent Study)
Domain
Subscriptions / Billing
Source
Frontline Support Insight

The Problem

While working in customer support, I noticed a recurring issue on shared Netflix accounts: when every streaming slot is in use, Netflix prompts the user to either upgrade the plan or stop streaming on another device. Often that other stream is running on a smart TV or an older device that no one can easily reach, or one that simply takes time to sign out of since it isn't immediate — in most cases around 10–15 minutes — so people upgrade just to keep watching, not because they actually want a bigger plan.

Customers who upgraded by accident then contact support to ask for a downgrade, a refund of the extra charge, and their original billing cycle back — creating avoidable frustration and support load.

User Pain Points

Customer
  • Accidentally upgrades the subscription
  • Faces unexpected charges
  • Billing date shifts without warning
  • Has to contact support to undo it
Business
  • Higher support contact volume
  • Refund processing costs
  • Reduced customer trust
  • A poor experience despite no actual technical fault

Root Cause

The current flow offers an immediate upgrade path but almost no help resolving the actual conflict — a device that's already streaming. Users end up making a subscription decision under time pressure, instead of being helped to simply free up a slot.

Proposed Solution

Netflix already allows users to remotely sign out of active devices. However, on older TVs and streaming devices, the sign-out request can take 10–15 minutes to process. During this delay, users often upgrade their plan just to continue watching, even though they only needed to wait a few minutes.

Option 1 — Improve Remote Sign-Out

After initiating a remote sign-out, Netflix should:

  • Display an estimated completion time (e.g., 10–15 minutes)
  • Explain that older TVs and streaming devices may take longer to process the request
  • Show a "Sign-out in progress" status
Maximum devices are streaming. Bedroom TV — Watching: Stranger Things (active 42 mins) Living Room TV — Watching: Wednesday [ End Bedroom Stream ] [ Keep Streaming Here ] Remote sign-out initiated. This device will stop streaming in approximately 10–15 minutes. Older TVs and streaming devices may take longer to process remote sign-out requests.
Option 2 — Join the Queue

Provide a "Notify me when a stream becomes available" option. Once an active stream ends, Netflix automatically signs in the waiting device and notifies the user.

Option 3 — Upgrade Only If Needed

Keep the Upgrade Plan option, but present it after the above alternatives so users can first resolve the issue using their existing subscription before upgrading.

Expected Impact

  • Fewer accidental upgrades and less billing confusion
  • Faster resolution, more transparency for the customer
  • Fewer support contacts, refunds, and operational costs for the business
  • Higher trust, without removing Netflix's ability to upsell to users who genuinely want more simultaneous streams

Success Metrics

  • Reduction in accidental upgrades and 24-hour downgrade requests
  • Reduction in refund requests
  • CSAT and NPS movement
  • Support contact rate for streaming-limit issues
  • Adoption rate of the Remote Sign Out feature
